# Independent domain-probe audit for the signal/noise encoder (2026-10-14)

## Question and answer

A near-chance AUC for the adversarial discriminator does **not** imply that the
encoder representation is domain-invariant.  On the same held-out MC/data events,
a newly fitted logistic regression on frozen final-layer encoder features often
achieves AUC 0.85–0.93 while the training discriminator reports AUC 0.52–0.65.

This is a targeted pilot on the **single-head** signal/noise model initialized
from `noise_sig_experiments/k_nsol_labelneq0_da_hs128_k0p0001/best_mc_2020.ckpt`.
It is not a reproduction of the paper's final multi-task signal/noise +
time-residual model and should not be substituted for its published score-W1
result without an exact-protocol rerun.

## Protocol

- Inputs: complete raw-hit events from the 2020 MC, experimental HDF5, each
  passing at least 8 raw hits on at least 2 strings.  Experimental features are
  renormalized to the MC normalization.  Sequences are truncated/padded to a
  fixed 128 hits; <0.2% of sampled events exceed 128 hits.
- Fine-tuning: 7,500 MC and 7,500 experimental events, 5 epochs, 96-event
  batches, main LR 1e-4.  The MC examples were drawn from a pre-existing 2020
  **test** subset, but are disjoint by event ID from the events used to fit and
  evaluate the probes.  These fine-tuned checkpoints are for a diagnostic
  study, not a new production-model test score.
- Probe: after every epoch freeze the encoder; fit `StandardScaler` +
  `LogisticRegression(C=1)` on 1,800 MC and 1,800 experimental events; evaluate
  on another disjoint 1,800 + 1,800.  The experimental fit/evaluation events
  come from HDF5 `val`/`test`; the MC split uses disjoint IDs in the same
  pre-existing test sample.  Report a muatm-only probe separately (600 or 900
  MC and the same number of experimental events per split).
- The primary probe uses a **masked** average of valid hit embeddings.  We also
  measured the model's original unmasked pooling.  The original discriminator
  pools over padded tokens (`hidden_states.mean(1)`), but that does not explain
  away the independent probe result: both pooled versions are highly separable.
- MC task precision/recall uses GT signal label `label != 0` and threshold
  `p(signal) >= 0.5` on the held-out probe-test MC events.  The paper-like
  variant uses 2:1:1 muatm:nuatm:nue2 and random training-time z inversion.
- The **new pilot** optimizes `L_hit + L_domain` with GRL scale `alpha` and an
  independently adjustable discriminator LR.  The historical implementation
  instead optimized `L_hit + k L_domain` with GRL scale `alpha`, multiplying the
  encoder domain gradient by `k*alpha` and the head gradient by `k`.  For the
  starting checkpoint, `k=1e-4` and `alpha=1`.

## Five-epoch sweep (1:1:1 MC, no z inversion)

| Setting (GRL alpha / discriminator LR) | Head separability AUC | Independent AUC mixed | Independent AUC muatm | Hit precision / recall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| No DA, masked pooling (0 / 1e-3) | 0.916 | 0.931 | 0.910 | 0.952 / 0.966 |
| DA, unmasked pooling (0.1 / 1e-3) | 0.646 | 0.908 | 0.857 | 0.922 / 0.960 |
| DA, masked pooling (0.1 / 1e-3) | 0.527 | 0.912 | 0.868 | 0.945 / 0.954 |
| DA, masked pooling (0.1 / 3e-3) | 0.559 | 0.888 | 0.838 | 0.954 / 0.959 |
| DA, masked pooling (0.3 / 1e-3) | 0.541 | 0.854 | 0.815 | 0.917 / 0.932 |
| DA, masked pooling (0.3 / 3e-3) | 0.524 | 0.879 | 0.857 | 0.953 / 0.958 |

The 0.1 / 3e-3 setting reduces mixed-probe AUC by 0.043 (paired 95% bootstrap
CI for DA minus control: [-0.052, -0.035]); for muatm-only, by 0.072
([-0.090, -0.056]).  Its mixed AUC after five epochs was 0.888, 0.898, and
0.904 over three ordering seeds; this still leaves substantial domain
information.  Increasing only GRL alpha further reduced probe AUC but caused
a much larger hit-task penalty.

## Closer-to-paper mixture and z inversion

| Setting | Head separability AUC | Independent AUC mixed | Independent AUC muatm | Hit precision / recall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2:1:1 + z flip, no DA | 0.890 | 0.922 | 0.895 | 0.953 / 0.953 |
| 2:1:1 + z flip, DA (0.1 / 3e-3) | 0.646 | 0.913 | 0.884 | 0.953 / 0.951 |
| Same task mixture, separate muatm-only domain stream, no DA | 0.886 | 0.920 | 0.891 | 0.945 / 0.959 |
| Same, separate muatm-only domain stream, DA | 0.609 | 0.919 | 0.894 | 0.946 / 0.955 |

For the 2:1:1 pilot, the mixed-probe AUC reduction is only 0.0083
(paired 95% CI [-0.0134, -0.0034]), and muatm-only is 0.0110
([-0.0182, -0.0028]).  With the separate muatm DA stream, the muatm-probe
difference is +0.0026 (CI [-0.0057, 0.0110]): no demonstrated reduction.
At epoch 1 of the 2:1:1 pilot, the discriminator AUC was 0.515 while the
independent probe still achieved 0.909.

## Interpretation and next scientific check

There is a measurable but strongly protocol-dependent reduction of linear
domain separability in one pilot, **not** domain invariance.  The discriminator
can become confused while the frozen encoder retains accessible domain
information.  Faster discriminator learning can help more than simply raising
the adversarial gradient, but the effective sample mixture and physical
composition are dominant.  A lower Wasserstein distance between output-score
distributions is a distinct claim and can coexist with a high representation
probe AUC; these experiments do not refute that result in the article.

Before changing the article's claim, repeat this probe on the exact final
multi-task checkpoint, its original train/validation splits and cuts, and the
same MC/data sample used for the published W1 plot.  Report head and probe
AUC across epochs, a muatm-conditioned probe, hit precision/recall, and W1 on
the **same** sample.  Do not select an epoch using the held-out probe-test set.
